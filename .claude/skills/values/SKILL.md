# Cameron's Values & Work Preferences

Reference these when evaluating companies, writing cover letters, crafting questions for interviewers, or assessing fit.

## What Cameron values

### Impact over headcount
Small teams where individual contributions visibly move the product. Ideal company size is small to medium — startups through growth-stage. Big-company bureaucracy is a dealbreaker.

### Product voice
Wants a seat at the table for product direction and vision, not just executing tickets. Engineering should inform what gets built, not just how.

### AI-forward environment
Strongly prefers companies that are leaning into AI tooling and workflows. At minimum, an environment where Cameron can drive that adoption and demonstrate the value himself.

### High trust and autonomy
Thrives when given a goal and the freedom to figure out how to hit it. Micromanagement is a non-starter.

### Work ethic with boundaries
Happy to put in long hours when the work demands it — not afraid of a crunch. But weekends and vacation time are protected. Sustainable pace matters over the long run.

## How to apply these

**When evaluating a company:** Flag alignment or misalignment with these values in fit assessments. A role at a 10,000-person company with rigid process is a poor fit even if the tech stack matches perfectly.

**When writing cover letters:** Weave in genuine enthusiasm for the aspects that align — don't just list values, show why this specific company hits them.

**When crafting interview questions:** Help Cameron ask questions that surface whether these values are real at the company. "How much input does engineering have on the roadmap?" tells you more than "What's the culture like?"

**When assessing red flags:** Watch for signals that contradict these values — heavy process overhead, "we're like a family" (often means no boundaries), engineering treated as a cost center, or a culture that's skeptical of AI tooling.
